## 1. Which credential you actually want

You said you weren't sure. Short answer: **ISA Certified Arborist**. The other two you asked about
aren't alternatives to it — they sit on top of it.

| Credential | What it is | Prerequisite | Verdict for you |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ISA Certified Arborist** | The core credential. 200-question exam over 10 domains. | 3 yrs practical experience *or* degree + reduced experience | **This is the one.** Everything else is downstream. |
| **Certified Arborist Municipal Specialist** | Adds urban/community forestry scope — street trees, ROW management, inventories, policy, public relations | Must already hold Certified Arborist | Very close to CB3 street-tree work, but it's a *later* step. Can't be your January exam. |
| **TRAQ** (Tree Risk Assessment Qualification) | A *qualification*, not a certification — a two-day taught course plus assessment | Certified Arborist, BCMA, a relevant degree, or a national arboriculture certification | Not a self-study exam you sit in January. Worth doing after CA if you take on risk assessment. Renews every 5 years, no CEUs. |

**Recommended path:** Certified Arborist (Jan 2027) → TRAQ once you hold it → Municipal Specialist
later if the Ecology Center's work moves further toward street-tree program management.

The rest of this document is about the Certified Arborist exam.

## 2. Eligibility — check this before anything else

You need **one** of:

- **3+ years of full-time practical arboriculture experience.** ISA counts one year as
  **≈1,795 hours**, so the real bar is ~5,385 hours.
- **A 2-year associate degree** in a related field with at least 2 arboriculture courses → **2 years**
  practical experience.
- **A 4-year bachelor's degree** in arboriculture, horticulture, landscape architecture, urban
  forestry, or forestry with at least 4 arboriculture courses → **1 year** practical experience.
- Registered/Industry Recognized Apprenticeship Programs may be accepted as equivalency.

**What counts as qualifying experience:** practical application of pruning, fertilization,
installation and establishment, diagnosis and treatment of tree problems, cabling and bracing,
climbing, and other directly arboricultural services.

**This matters for you specifically:** much of the stewardship work behind this dashboard —
plantings, structural pruning, health assessment, tree inventory and census work, guard
installation — is squarely qualifying experience. And **supervised volunteer work counts** if you
can document responsibilities and hours. That is a real opening if your paid title doesn't read as
"arborist," but it puts the burden on documentation.

**Action now, not later:** applications require documentation of experience, and reference
letters from current or former employers are accepted. Start collecting these in August — chasing
a supervisor for a letter in December is how January slips to March.

## 3. How the exam is actually delivered (this drives your whole timeline)

The single most important logistical fact: **there is no "January exam date."** ISA delivers the
Certified Arborist exam by computer at **Pearson VUE** test centers, and **you pick the date and
location yourself** after ISA approves your application. Results come back immediately at the end
of the session.

The constraint is the **90-day authorization window**: once your application is approved, you get
90 days to schedule and sit the exam. Apply too early and the window closes before January.

Paper-based sittings still run at some chapter events, but computer-based is the flexible option
and the one that fits a self-directed January target.

## 4. Working timeline backwards from January 2027

| When | Do this |
|---|---|
| **August 2026** | Confirm which eligibility path you qualify under. Start collecting hours documentation and request reference letters. Order the *Arborists' Certification Study Guide, 4th ed.* Decide on ISA membership (§6). |
| **Aug – Oct 2026** | Study the heavy domains first — Safe Work Practices (15%), Pruning (14%), Tree Biology and Tree Risk (11% each) are **51% of the exam** between them. |
| **~mid-to-late October 2026** | **Submit the application.** Allow a few weeks for review. Targeting approval around **1 November** puts your 90-day window at roughly **1 Nov 2026 – 30 Jan 2027** — January sits comfortably inside it. |
| **On approval** | You'll get an email with a scheduling link. **Book your Pearson VUE seat immediately**, even for a date weeks out — NYC-area centers fill, and booking early costs nothing. |
| **Nov – Dec 2026** | Full domain sweep plus timed practice exams. |
| **January 2027** | Sit the exam. Aim for the first half of the month so a retake still fits inside the authorization window if needed. |

**Don't apply in August.** A 90-day window opened in August expires in November.

**Scheduling conflict worth knowing about:** the regional ISA prep courses in this area tend to run
**February through spring** — the NYS Urban Forestry Council course ran a 5-session cohort starting
25 February 2026. A 2027 cohort on the same cadence would start *after* your January exam. So it's
either **self-study for January**, or **shift the exam to spring 2027** to take a prep course
first. Worth deciding deliberately rather than discovering in February.

## 7. Exam format and domain weighting

- **200 multiple-choice questions**
- **3.5 hours** (210 minutes)
- **Passing score ~76%**, computed from *weighted* domain scores — the domain average will not
  equal your overall score
- Immediate results at a computer-based sitting

| Domain | Weight |
|---|---|
| Safe Work Practices | 15% |
| Pruning | 14% |
| Tree Biology | 11% |
| Tree Risk | 11% |
| Tree Identification and Selection | 9% |
| Installation and Establishment | 9% |
| Diagnosis and Treatment | 9% |
| Trees and Construction | 9% |
| Soil Management | 7% |
| Urban Forestry | 6% |

Weights come from the current exam content outline and change when ISA completes a new job task
analysis — check the outline is current when you apply.

Note the shape of it: **Safe Work Practices is the single heaviest domain at 15%**, ahead of
Pruning. Candidates coming from a stewardship and urban-forestry background often under-study
climbing, aerial lift, electrical hazard, and PPE material because it's the least familiar. Urban
Forestry, probably your strongest area, is the *lightest* domain at 6%. Budget study time by
weight, not by comfort.

## 8. Study materials and NY-area prep

**Primary text — get this one:** *Arborists' Certification Study Guide*, 4th edition (Lilly,
Bassett, Komen, Purcell; ISA). Each chapter has learning objectives, key terms, a workbook section,
challenge questions, and sample test questions. Prep courses in this region are structured chapter
by chapter around it.

**Also useful:** the ISA Best Management Practices series (the study guide is aligned to BMP
revisions), and the official exam content outline as a study checklist.

**NY-area courses (verify 2026–27 schedules directly — all these sites were blocked here):**

- **New York Botanical Garden** — ISA Arborist Certification Exam Prep Course, works chapter by
  chapter through the study guide. Closest to you geographically.
- **NYS Urban Forestry Council** — 5-session ISA Prep Course, ~$629 for all five including the
  study guide. Ran late Feb 2026; watch for the 2027 cohort.
- **Cornell Cooperative Extension (Dutchess)** — ISA Certified Arborist Training Program.
- **NYS Arborists, ISA Chapter** — the state chapter; certification info and education calendar.
- **Long Island Arboricultural Association** — education and certification programming.

## 9. After you pass

Certification runs on a **three-year cycle**. To recertify you need **30 CEUs** across the three
years, tied to the 10 tested domains, plus a recertification fee of **~$220** and a **~$40**
processing fee **[CONFIRM]**. Chapter conferences, workshops, and webinars are the usual sources —
another argument for chapter membership.
